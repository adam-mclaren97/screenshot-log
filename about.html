Vision Statement

About This Collection

Once a day, without scrolling to find something better, I capture the first thing X puts in my feed. I keep the whole screen, because what surrounds a post is also something the platform decided I should see.

Working Title

My working title is The Whole Frame. The title names my capture rule: I screenshot the entire screen rather than cropping the post that appears in my feed. A post is never alone on a screen, and what surrounds it is also something the platform decided I should see.

Thesis

I believe the first post X places in my For You feed will reveal that the platform constructs a version of the world organized less around importance and more around its ability to secure my attention. I expect more than half of my captures to make an emotional demand — to provoke or to alarm — and the remaining categories, amusement, distraction, and selling, to function as intervals rather than filler. If those intervals cluster immediately after the most demanding captures, that is evidence for the mechanism. If they fall randomly across twelve weeks, it is not. I do not treat "information" as a separate category, because every post is information. What matters is what the information is trying to do, and my five categories are meant to name that.

My project began as an attempt to document rage bait and determine how long controversial stories remained relevant before the algorithm replaced them. That approach risked becoming circular. If I searched only for outrage, I would inevitably produce a collection proving that outrage exists. Instead, I will surrender part of the selection process to the platform by capturing whatever it presents first. Over twelve weeks, I want to test whether the resulting collection supports my expectation that X repeatedly moves between provocation, alarm, amusement, distraction, and selling. My thesis could be wrong. The first posts may be mostly ordinary, or their apparent patterns may reflect my own attention more than the platform’s priorities. That uncertainty is part of what interests me.

The Capture Rule

Once each day, I will open X’s For You feed, refresh it once, and capture the first complete item displayed without scrolling to select something more interesting. If the post doesn’t fit into my screen, I will still take a single screenshot without scrolling and note it in the entry for the post. Where a post gets cut off also reveals information about the algorithm and what I’m being shown. Additionally, if the first post is a video, I will capture whatever frame is there and not seek out a specific frame to screenshot.

A qualifying item can be an original post, repost, recommended video, public advertisement, or other complete piece of feed content. The screenshot will preserve as much of the surrounding frame as possible, including the time, engagement totals, navigation elements, live banners, advertisements, and portions of adjacent posts. These details may seem incidental at first, but they can reveal how different forms of information coexist within the same feed. Notifications, direct messages, and other private communications do not count and will not be captured.

A trending-topic page, search result, account page, or post I deliberately navigate toward almost counts, but it is excluded because my project concerns what X selects before I begin making choices. The hard case will be an advertisement, banner, or partially visible post appearing first. Advertisements will count because they are part of what the platform has chosen to foreground. A live-event banner or navigation prompt will remain visible as context, but I will move only far enough to display the first complete feed item. If the app resumes in the middle of an older session, I will return to the top of the For You feed and refresh once before capturing.

Plan of Capture Action

My target will be the For You feed in the X mobile application. I will make one capture per day, seven days per week, producing at least seven captures each week. To preserve possible differences in time of day, I will alternate capture windows. On Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Sunday, I will capture between 8:00 a.m. and noon. On Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday, I will capture between 6:00 and 10:00 p.m. This schedule is structured enough to create comparisons but flexible enough to survive a busy week at work and school. Taking the screenshot is easy, but I need to dedicate the time to properly annotate each capture. Each Sunday, I will write up that week’s seven entries rather than trying to finish each one on the day of the capture.

Each capture will be logged with its date, time, content category, visible engagement, emotional or rhetorical demand, and whether I followed the account. I will also note significant surrounding details, including advertisements and repeated stories. If I miss a day, I will record the gap and its reason rather than manufacture a replacement capture. Taking two screenshots the following day would not recreate what the platform presented during the missed period and would falsely suggest uninterrupted observation.

What This Should Do for a Reader

I want readers to question what “first” means in an algorithmically arranged environment. The first post is not necessarily the most important event, the most accurate account, or even something the user consciously requested. It is a platform-generated opening statement. By placing many of those opening statements side by side, I hope to reveal recurring strategies that would be difficult to recognize one post at a time.

When someone reaches the end of the collection, I want them to feel that they have examined a personalized media environment rather than simply scrolled through an assortment of posts. The collection should make visible how quickly a feed can move from breaking news to political identity, humor, tragedy, entertainment, and advertising, while presenting each item as the next natural thing to pay attention to.

The intended effect is not simply to convince readers that social media is harmful or that every post is rage bait. I want them to pay closer attention to the transitions, framing devices, engagement measurements, advertisements, and emotional shifts that make a feed feel continuous. Ideally, visitors will leave wondering what version of the world their own feed constructs for them, and how much of that world they have mistaken for an unfiltered reality.

Guardrails

I will not capture direct messages, private accounts, personal notifications, or any other person’s private communication. Public posts may appear because they are the subject of the project, but I will obscure the identities of nonpublic individuals when their names are not relevant to the analysis, and I will note this action in the entry. The content of a post is what I am analyzing. I will not include graphic material or anything I would be unwilling to display to the class. If prohibited or intensely private material appears first, I will log that an exclusion occurred without preserving or displaying the material itself.
